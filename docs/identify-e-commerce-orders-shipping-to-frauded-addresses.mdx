title: Identify e-commerce orders shipping to frauded addresses
---
## Introduction

One of the more creative uses of Datafiniti is using our property data to track potentially fraudulent e-commerce orders.  Fraudsters may attempt to ship packages to a vacant home and pick up those packages from those houses because they know no one is living there.  Online retailers can use our property data to check the shipping addresses against the addresses of recently listed or sold homes to flag potentially fraudulent orders.

Here's a brief overview of how this process works.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Collect a list of recent shipping addresses" title-type="p">
    As new orders come in, collect the shipping addresses on each order.  That list might look something like:

    ```text
    Address, City, State, Zip
    1023 Jollyville Rd, Houston, TX, 77079
    451 Webber Ln, Austin, TX 78721
    200 Tennyson Ave, Pittsburgh, PA, 15213
    ```

    <Callout kind="tip" collapsed="true">
      **Address normalization**

      Datafiniti normalizes street designations to standardized abbreviations, such as converting `street` to `st` and `drive` to `dr`. Use the normalized form of an address for exact matching against Datafiniti property data. Learn more in [Normalized Property Addresses](/docs/normalized-address-data).
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check an MLS address against Datafiniti" title-type="p">
    Pass each address (provided by your or an MLS network) to the Datafiniti Property API and check the status of the property.  Your query will look like:

    ```json
    {
      "query": "address:\"200 Tennyson Ave\" AND city:Pittsburgh AND province:PA AND postalCode:15213"
    }
    ```

    If there's a match, you'll get data back on the most recent status of the property.  The data in the returned property record will look like:

    ```json
    "mostRecentStatus": "For Sale",
    "mostRecentStatusDate": "2023-03-28T15:34:20.727Z",
    ```
  </Step>

  <Step title="Flag properties recently listed or sold" title-type="p">
    If the address matches a property that is recently listed (`For Sale`) or sold (`Sold`), then you can decide to flag that corresponding order as potentially fraudulent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Flag orders shipping to business addresses

Vacant homes aren't the only shipping-address risk.  Fraudsters also route stolen-card orders to commercial locations disguised as residences — freight forwarders, reshipping "drops," and commercial mail-receiving agencies (CMRAs) — or to addresses that don't correspond to a real delivery point at all.  The `businessesAtAddress` field lets you confirm whether an address a customer entered as a home is actually a place of business, adding a second layer of fraud coverage on top of the vacant-home check.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the shipping address for a business" title-type="p">
    Reuse the same property query from above.  In the returned record, inspect the `businessesAtAddress` field, which lists any businesses Datafiniti has seen operating at that address:

    ```json
    "businessesAtAddress": [
      {
        "name": "Alderbrook Boutique",
        "key": "us/me/eastbrook/1212eastbrookrd/alderbrookboutique",
        "dateSeen": "2026-09-26T00:22:11Z"
      }
    ]
    ```
  </Step>

  <Step title="Flag residential orders tied to a business" title-type="p">
    If a shipping address entered as a home comes back with one or more entries in `businessesAtAddress`, treat it as a commercial location rather than a residence.  Pair it with `propertyType` to separate a genuine home from a storefront, mailbox service, or freight forwarder.  When no business has ever been seen at the address and the address matches no property record, that absence can point to a fake or non-deliverable address worth holding for review.

    <Callout kind="tip" collapsed="true">
      **Weigh home-based businesses**

      A legitimate home-based business can also appear in `businessesAtAddress`, so treat this field as one signal among several (order value, billing/shipping mismatch, vacancy status) rather than an automatic decline.
    </Callout>
  </Step>
</Steps>

A record flagged by the business-address check looks like this — the address was entered as a residence, but Datafiniti shows a business operating there:

```json
{
  "address": "1212 Eastbrook Rd",
  "city": "Eastbrook",
  "province": "ME",
  "postalCode": "04634",
  "businessesAtAddress": [
    {
      "name": "Alderbrook Boutique",
      "key": "us/me/eastbrook/1212eastbrookrd/alderbrookboutique",
      "dateSeen": "2026-09-26T00:22:11Z"
    }
  ]
}
```

A genuine residence returns no business at the address:

```json
{
  "address": "451 Webber Ln",
  "city": "Austin",
  "province": "TX",
  "postalCode": "78721"
}

```
